# Proposal: microphone-driven mouth blendshapes

Design decided and built: all five pieces (the live stage, the measurements, the config, the panel, the recording sidecar, and the calibration wizard are in `docs/reference/audio.md`). This document is the record of the design: the decisions, the pieces in order, and what verifies each one. A reader starting from it should not need the conversation that produced it.

## Goal

MikanTrack's face comes from the iFacialMocap phone stream alone (`docs/reference/face.md`). A session without the phone has a still mouth. This work adds an audio stage that listens to a microphone and drives the jaw, mouth, cheek puff, and tongue blendshapes from the voice, through the same `TrackingFrameResult::face` columns, the same face map, and the same `/VMC/Ext/Blend/Val` output the phone uses. Two backends sit behind one stage interface, and the user picks one:

- `wav2arkit`: the `wav2arkit_cpu` ONNX model (Apache 2.0, a fused export of the wav2vec2-base-960h encoder and the LAM Audio2Expression decoder). Raw 16 kHz mono float audio in, 52 ARKit weights at 30 fps out. About 0.4 GB of weights, roughly 45 ms of CPU per second of audio.
- `mfcc`: a C++ port of HeadAudio (MIT), the reimplementation of uLipSync's method: MFCC feature vectors classified against Gaussian prototypes by Mahalanobis distance into the 15 Oculus visemes, which a fixed table turns into the same ARKit columns. A 14 kB model, about 0.6 ms of CPU per second of audio, about 100 ms of its own latency (the gate, the six-hop vote, the crossfade), language-agnostic once calibrated to the speaker.

Japanese is a shipping requirement: both backends must handle it, measured by the bench before anything goes live.

## Decisions

- **Bench before live.** The first deliverable is `--viseme-bench`, a headless command that runs a backend over a wav file under the live window policy, writes the 30 fps mouth weights as JSON, and reports per-run inference milliseconds. Quality is judged by playing the JSON back on the avatar in the 3D scene and by the chunked-versus-offline diff below. The live stage lands only after the numbers pass.

- **Own audio thread on the CPU execution provider.** `AudioService` (`src/Audio/`) owns a dedicated thread that drains the capture ring, keeps the rolling window, runs the selected backend, and publishes the newest `MouthSample` latest-wins behind a mutex, the shape `FaceService` already uses. The vision thread reads it once per iteration beside the face sample. The wav2arkit session is created on the audio thread with the CPU provider and four intra-op threads, so the single DirectML queue stays with the hand and body models and a slow audio run can never hitch the vision loop. The GPU was measured and rejected: the shipped graph does not load on DirectML, and a patched export runs slower there than on the CPU (audio.md). `OnnxSession` grows no new surface for this: its `"cpu"` provider string already exists, and the one-thread-per-session rule it documents holds because the audio thread is that session's only user.

- **Fixed window policy: 100 ms hop over a 1 s window, 4 threads.** Every 100 ms the backend runs over the last 1 s of audio and the last 3 frames of its output are emitted. The design started at 2 s (the LAM decoder's own context) on 2 threads; the bench measured that at 162 ms per run, unable to keep up, and found the window length makes no difference to the chunked drift, so the window shrank to 1 s on 4 threads at 50 ms per run. The hop, window, and thread count are arguments of the bench, not panel knobs. A `lookaheadFrames` setting holds frames back from the tail of each run for context at 33 ms of latency each; the bench measured 0, 2, 4, and 6, and the default is two, a third less drift for 67 ms.

- **The mouth group is the only thing audio drives.** Audio cannot see eyes or brows, so the backend's other outputs are discarded. The group is the 4 jaw columns, the 23 mouth columns, `cheekPuff`, and `tongueOut`, 29 of the 52, listed once in `AudioTypes.h` and covered by a self-test.

- **Phone wins while streaming.** `VisionThread::applyFaceToOutput` applies the audio mouth group only when no phone face sample is live. No blend, no source combo. A frame no camera carried still goes out when a new mouth sample arrives, through the face-only output path generalized to any face source.

- **SDL2 audio captures the microphone.** `SDL_GetNumAudioDevices(1)` and `SDL_GetAudioDeviceName` fill the panel's device combo. `SDL_OpenAudioDevice` opens the chosen device for capture asking for 16 kHz mono float32 and lets SDL convert, so the backends never see another format. The callback copies into an SPSC ring (`moodycamel::ReaderWriterQueue` of fixed blocks, as the camera slots do), and the audio thread drains it. A level meter in dBFS RMS per hop and a noise gate threshold live beside the device combo. Below the gate both backends emit the neutral mouth, eased over one hop so a gate edge does not snap the jaw.

- **Settings persist per project.** `AudioConfig` on `AppConfig::audio` under `audio` in `project.json`, beside `face`: `enabled`, `deviceName` (re-resolved at open, falling back to the default device with a log line), `backend` (`wav2arkit` or `mfcc`), `gainDb`, `gateDbfs`, and `profilePath` for the MFCC profile (empty means the shipped model). A config refresh pushes it through `App::applyAudioConfig` the way the face config goes, and the recording header's snapshot describes it.

- **Both backends behind one interface.** `IMouthBackend` takes a window of 16 kHz samples and returns mouth frames at 30 fps plus a ready flag and a reason string (model file missing, profile unreadable). Backends are pure over the window; the window policy (`MouthWindow`) decides which frames are new and a one-euro filter per column (`MouthFilter`) smooths the emitted frames after that, since the LAM post-processing (its Savitzky-Golay pass and symmetrization) lives in Python and is not in the fused graph. Keeping the filter out of the backend is what lets the bench compare chunked and whole-clip output. The wav2arkit backend wraps the ONNX session, created with `OnnxSession::create`'s intra-op thread cap (the one addition to that class). The MFCC backend is the HeadAudio processor ported stage for stage: pre-emphasis, 512-sample blocks at a 256 hop, Hamming window, a radix-2 FFT of its own (so the vectors match HeadAudio's to float precision, which `--test-audio` checks against vectors its JavaScript computed), mel filterbank, 12 MFCCs plus log energy, tanh compression, HeadAudio's energy gate in front of the classifier (its shipped silence prototype matches digital silence alone, so without the gate every pause reads as speech), Mahalanobis distance to each prototype, a majority vote over the last six hops, and its 100 ms crossfade between visemes. The polyphase downsampler is not ported, since the stage's input is already 16 kHz. The binary model format is read from HeadAudio's `training.mjs`, and the shipped `model-en-mixed.bin` is committed under `resources/audio/` with a NOTICE.md entry.

- **A fixed viseme table.** The 15 Oculus visemes map onto ARKit mouth columns by a constant table in `VisemeTable.cpp` (`aa` drives `jawOpen`, `PP` drives `mouthClose` and both `mouthPress`, `ou` drives `mouthPucker` and `mouthFunnel`, `E` drives `mouthStretch` and `jawOpen` at half, and so on through the set), each row a weighted sum clamped to one. The table is covered by a self-test and is the only place the two backends differ in what they hand the merge.

- **Calibration wizard for the MFCC backend.** `eWizardKind::AudioCalibration` on the `WizardHost` records six prompts of three seconds each with the countdown the other wizards use: the vowels `aa`, `ih`, `ou`, `ee`, `oh` held, then room silence. The vowel prototypes are fit as Gaussian means and covariances of the MFCC vectors, the silence prompt seeds the gate default, and the nine consonant visemes keep the shipped model's prototypes, so a profile is a vowel overlay. It is written to `<project>/audio/profile.bin` and `AudioConfig::profilePath` points at it. The prompts are localized through the `audio` section, so the Japanese walkthrough reads the five Japanese vowels.

- **Model and clips fetched by `InitialSetup_x64.bat`.** Two more curl lines fetch `wav2arkit_cpu.onnx` and `wav2arkit_cpu.onnx.data` from Hugging Face into `models/`. Two bench clips, one English and one Japanese, synthesized once with Kokoro, are published as assets of a `bench-audio-1` release on the MikanTrack GitHub repository and fetched into `resources/audio/bench/`. The backend reports itself unavailable with the model file missing, and the bench skips a clip that is absent rather than failing.

- **Recordings carry the audio.** A recording writes `<name>.wav` beside its JSONL, 16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM, written by the audio thread into its own bounded queue and a writer the way `TrackingRecorder` works. The header's `audio` block carries the file name, the backend, the config snapshot, and the first sample's time on the `steadyNowMs` clock, so a frame's time indexes into the wav. Each frame also records the 29 mouth weights the live run emitted. Replay stays outside the checksum contract, like `BodyPoseSolver`: with `--replay-audio` it re-runs the selected backend from the wav after the checksum point and reports the per-column diff against the recorded weights, so a backend change can be A/B'd against old recordings.

- **The Audio panel.** A new dock window in the left column with Device, Tracking, and Settings, toggled from the View menu like Settings and Avatar. Top to bottom: the enable checkbox, the backend combo, the device combo with a refresh button, the level meter with the gate slider and the gain slider, a status block (device open and its rate, backend ready or the reason it is not, inference milliseconds as a one-second EMA, which source owns the mouth: phone, audio, or gated), and for the MFCC backend the Calibrate button with the profile path. Strings go in a new `audio` section of `en.json` and `ja.json`, the title under `windows.audio`.

## Pieces in order

Each piece is one reviewable commit on the `viseme` branch.

1. **Inference core and bench on wav2arkit.** Built. `src/Audio/` with `AudioTypes.h` (the mouth group, `MouthSample`), `IMouthBackend`, `Wav2ArkitBackend`, `MouthWindow`, `MouthFilter`, `WavFile`, and `--viseme-bench` plus `--render-mouth` in `src/Tests/`. The setup script fetches the model and the two clips. `--test-audio` covers the mouth group list, the model's column table, the window policy's frame accounting with and without lookahead, the wav reader, the filter, and the backend on a synthetic tone. NOTICE.md carries the model's and the clips' provenance.

2. **The MFCC backend into the same bench.** Built. `MfccBackend`, `MfccFeatures`, `VadGate`, `VisemeModel`, `VisemeTable`, `VisemeTypes`, `resources/audio/model-en-mixed.bin`, and `--backend` on the bench. `--test-audio` covers the MFCC chain against vectors computed from HeadAudio's JavaScript on the same input, the model reader and classifier on the shipped model, the table, the gate, and the backend. NOTICE.md carries the port and the model. The measurements are in audio.md.

3. **The live stage and the panel.** Built. `AudioService` with the SDL2 capture, the audio thread, the gate and gain, `AudioConfig`, `App::applyAudioConfig`, the vision thread's merge in `applyFaceToOutput`, the Audio panel, the dock slot, the View menu entry, and the localization section. `--loc-test` covers the strings. The live check is the avatar's mouth in the 3D scene and in VNyan over VMC, with the phone on and off.

4. **Recording and replay.** Built. The wav sidecar and its writer, the header block, the per-frame weights, and `--replay-audio`. The recording self-test covers the header round trip and the wav index arithmetic.

5. **The calibration wizard.** Built. The E viseme's English prompt is "eh, as in bed" rather than `ee`, since the shipped model files `ee` under the I viseme. `AudioCalibrationWizard` under `src/UI/`, the prototype fit, the profile writer and reader, the `profilePath` plumbing, and the Calibrate button. `--test-audio` covers the profile round trip and that a fit on synthetic vowel vectors classifies them back.

Docs land with their piece: a new `docs/reference/audio.md` (the stage, the backends, the window policy, the gate, the config, the panel, the wizard, the recording sidecar), the module map and threading model in `architecture.md`, the merge rule in `face.md`, the dependency and model tables in `build.md`, `commands.md`, `testing.md`, and the sidecar in `debugging.md`. README gains the microphone setup and the Audio panel.

## Verification

- The bench's gate for going live: on each clip, for each backend, the mean absolute difference per mouth column between the chunked output and a single whole-file run, and the per-run inference p50 and p95 on the CPU provider. The latency budget holds when p95 is under the 100 ms hop. The quality judgment is the avatar playing the JSON back, in English and in Japanese, with the measured verdict recorded in `LEARNINGS.md`.
- Each piece builds the `MikanTrack` target and runs the self-tests it touched. `--loc-test` runs with the panel piece.
- The live chain is checked once with a phone and once without: the mouth follows the voice in the 3D scene and in VNyan, the gate silences keyboard noise, the phone takes the mouth back when it streams, and a recording's wav replays through `--replay-audio` with a diff near zero on the same backend.
